# Code map

A quick map for agents. It is not a design document: behaviour is defined by the sources
and the tests. Rules live in `AGENTS.md`.

## App (`Scrollio/`)

| Folder | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `ScrollCore/` | Smooth scrolling, one path: `ScrollCore` takes the wheel event (tap callback, main thread) and adds max(Step, delta) × Speed to a target; `ScrollPoster` moves toward it on every display frame (CVDisplayLink thread, under its lock) with a lerp set by Duration (`ScrollMotion`, 60 Hz reference), a lead cap (`ScrollLeadLimiter`) and a soft start (`ScrollFilter`), and posts the frames in whole pixels, the fraction of each carried into the next (`ScrollPixelCarry`; frames slower than the dead zone, a speed in pixels per 60 Hz frame, are not posted; for a Duration longer than Smooth's the dead zone is smaller in proportion, `ScrollMotion.postingDeadZone` and `arrivalDeadZone`, so no Duration loses more of a scroll than Smooth or keeps it open longer after its last frame); `ScrollPhase` adds the trackpad-style phases. Everything about them is derived (AppKit 27.2, WebKit, Chromium and Gecko read in their code; WebKit measured in a WKWebView without a display), nothing was seen on a screen. A scroll that is mainly sideways carries no phases in any app (`ScrollPoster.isMainlySideways`: a sideways gesture is a swipe to the app; an event with both deltas keeps the running scroll's kind unless the other axis leads two to one, so a device that wobbles about the diagonal keeps its scroll). An up-down scroll goes out in one of two forms. (1) One touch until the scroll has run out, with no glide events at all, for every app that is not known to scroll by a glide's events (AppKit and UIKit scroll views follow the touch only and add a glide of their own after it, sized from the touch's last frames by their timestamps; a web view in such an app follows the touch whole): the touch is laid over the scroll as it runs with the switch off (the phase machine runs as it does then and only times the scroll, so the frames are the switch-off path's); "began" goes out with the wheel event, without distance; when the scroll runs out by itself, "ended" goes out `oneTouchRest` (0.2 s) after the touch's last frame by the frames' own times, so that AppKit's speed is stale and it adds nothing, and a wheel event in that rest goes on with the open touch (the scroll under it may be over by then: `resetMotionLocked` puts the motion back as the stop would, and the stop waits for the "ended"); a forced end (a click, a finger on the trackpad, a wheel event more than 30 pt away or in another app, Pause, a tap restart, the switch) posts "ended" at once and then the two-finger tap (`twoFingerTap`: scroll phase 128, then 8), which stops an app that glides on by itself. The one touch has an end watch (`ScrollEndWatch.swift`; `ScrollPoster.endWatch`, a protocol with the real reader `AccessibilityScrollEndWatch` in the app and a stand-in that sees mid-page at once in tests and the harness): an app holds a page stretched past its end for as long as a touch lasts (the owner, build 1674), so Scrollio reads the vertical scroll bar's value of the scroll area under the pointer (the roles on the way up and that value, nothing else; on the reader's own queue, a 0.15 s messaging timeout per element and 0.25 s for the process, a look that waited 0.25 s on the queue answers "not seen" unasked, only where `AXIsProcessTrusted()` is true, never a prompt; the poster calls it without its lock, a look per scroll and a read per display frame at most, and never waits). The touch opens with the answer "seen" and the frames before it are plain; not seen means no phases for that scroll (the switch-off scroll); at the end the scroll heads for, with the value seen to follow the frames, "ended" goes out at once with the clock's time and no tap and the rest of the motion is dropped (the app bounces by itself); wheel events that head further into that end are taken in while a look at the pointer no older than 0.3 s still finds the same bar at that end, for 4 s at most (`endHold`, the most careful part: it sends nothing for a wheel event; `endHoldHint` is the one Bool the switch-off path tests for it; a hold that went stale is ended on the main queue, `endHoldIfStale`); "at the end" is within a millionth of 0 or 1; a scroll that starts at the end gets a short touch (two moving frames), then plain frames, or the hold where an arrival was seen on that bar (`provenBar`: it is the bar that is proven, not the place; the reader numbers the bars its looks find, `ScrollEndReading.bar`, by `CFEqual`). The way up from the element under the pointer is `ScrollEndClimb.look`, a function over `ScrollEndElements` (what a look may ask of an app), so tests run it over a tree of their own: it never asks the application's element anything (the climb stops at the window, the menu bar, and at any element whose parent is the application: Chrome and Electron switch on their accessibility at the application's role, read in their source), takes a disabled bar for a view whose content fits, and has a second form (`readsRoles` false) that asks no element for its role before it has shown a vertical scroll bar. That form is for `ScrollUtils.TrackpadStyleTarget.nativeShell` (Chromium's frameworks without an application class in `Info.plist`: Dia, Arc; `isNativeShellAroundChromium`), where the view that holds a web page switches on Chromium's web accessibility when asked for its role: there the look (`ScrollEndClimb.carefulLook`) also gathers numbers that tell the page's view from the rest (`ScrollEndShellFacts.isThePage`: no children, large, the same element at a second hit test 40 pt away), and a scroll is a flick unless a look has said "not the page" about the place (`shellVerdict`: same app, 30 pt, "the page" 2 s and "not the page" 60 s after its last use; a flick asks one look per place, `askForShellLookLocked`); on that answer the flick is closed at once and the same scroll goes on as a one touch (`shellLookAnswered`, `enterOneTouchLocked`), which is plain frames unless a bar was seen. Two settings of the whole app say where trackpad-style works while the main switch is on (`Options.trackpadStyleInBrowsers`, on by default: the flick kinds and the native shell; `Options.trackpadStyleInMacApps`, off by default: the one touch; read in `configSnapshot` through two seams that only `ScrollPoster.shared` points at the settings, every other poster counts both as on): where one is off the form is that of the main switch off and nothing is asked of the app; their rows stand under the Experimental switch in Scrolling › Advanced while it is on (`syncTrackpadScopeRows`), and the settings file carries both. No look is made while an app's kind is not known (`ConfigSnapshot.looksWait`, `ScrollUtils.trackpadStyleKindIsKnown`). Where a look sees no bar (outside a native shell) the second way is the content watch (`ScrollPoster.ContentWatch`, `startContentWatchLocked`, `judgeContentLocked`; the reader keeps the element under the pointer and `readContent` says where it is): the scroll starts plain and the element's position is read once per display frame; moved with the frames (three readings, 48 pt, 0.75 to 1.25 of what was sent) opens the touch and remembers the place (`followsMemory`, 60 s); moved less than half of what was sent over 0.1 s and 30 pt is an arrival ("ended" at once, the rest dropped, and no hold: nothing is ever taken in on its strength); never open past `watchBlindLimit` without a reading; an element that is gone or 400 pt off is taken again under the pointer. None of the real reader ran against an app in a test; the scroll log's second line per one-touch scroll (`ScrollEndWatchLog.line`) says what it met. (2) A flick, for an app that scrolls by the glide's events and makes no glide of its own (`ScrollUtils.trackpadStyleTarget`: the Chromium engine, found by `resources.pak` in one of the app's frameworks, or by a browser's `app_mode_loader` for a web app installed from one; the Gecko engine, found by `Contents/MacOS/XUL`; looked into once per app off the main thread, when the app comes to the front (`ScrollCore.watchFrontApplication`, only while trackpad-style is on for it) or at its first wheel event, only in the Applications folders and /System; until the answer, and for an app elsewhere, the app counts as touch-only): "began" is the first frame that moves the page (`ScrollPhase.holdsWaitingBegan`; never a frame without distance), the touch lasts 45 ms from the wheel event and until two frames have moved the page, and "ended" is held until the glide's first frame and goes out right before it, in the same display frame (`endedWaitsForGlide`); when the wheel goes on more than 30 pt from the touch's place, the gesture is closed before the new event is stored, so its closing frames go out at its own place (`closeFlickGestureLocked`). The flick also for the browsers WebKit draws (`TrackpadStyleTarget.safari`: every bundle id that begins with `com.apple.Safari`, and the DuckDuckGo browser) where the hardware record comes off a frame, and no phases at all for them where it does not (before macOS 27, a failed self-test): with a record on "momentum began" WebKit replaces the glide (Safari with the flick: seen on the owner's screen, macOS 27.2, 2026-11-12; DuckDuckGo: the same engine, not seen). No phases at all for the apps where a wheel has to stay a wheel (`ScrollUtils.wheelOnlyBundleIdentifiers`: Maps, Find My, Calendar, iPhone Mirroring, Blender, the Godot editor; an id goes into these lists only from a source that can be named). Every frame that carries phases (`ScrollOutgoingFrame.isTrackpadStyle`; never Chrome's "ended" with the switch off) is written as a trackpad's event: its own time on an even grid (`advanceFrameStampLocked`, `stampLocked`: the display frame before plus the display's interval, from the clock only at a scroll's first frame, after a stall and for a frame from the main thread; the grid never goes back, it goes on by half steps while it is more than a frame ahead of the clock, so two frames of display frames that follow each other are at least half an interval apart; never twice the same, and never before the last scroll's), its own line and fixed-point deltas (a tenth of its pixels, written before the point delta), scroll count 0, never a touch phase and a momentum phase together, and without the wheel event's hardware record (`ScrollHardwareRecord`: `SLEventSetIOHIDEvent(frame, NULL)` by `dlsym`, on the frame's own copy; used only when a self-test passed, which runs once, at launch only when trackpad-style is on in the Scrolling pane or for some app (`trackpadStyleIsOnSomewhere`), else at the first wheel event whose frames carry phases, and never for a user who has it off; not tried at all before macOS 27, where passing NULL was never run: frames keep the record there and the `app` log says so, as a note). EVALUATION SWITCH, to be removed before this is released: `TrackpadStyleEvaluation` (the default `trackpadStyleEvaluationOldLongTouch`, read once at launch and logged in the `app` log, no setting and in no settings list) brings back the old long touch for comparison (a touch while the wheel turns, the rest as glide events). Trackpad (and Magic Mouse) events pass untouched: `ScrollEvent.isTrackpad` tells them by a touch or glide phase, or a swipe count on a continuous event (a wheel turned during a trackpad glide carries the count too, but is not continuous). `ScrollHandover` decides who moves the page when the two overlap: a wheel event during a trackpad glide drops the rest of that glide (its "ended" goes on), and a finger that begins to scroll stops the wheel's glide. The sideways wheel (the thumb wheel of an MX Master, or a wheel that tilts) has its own settings (`Options.thumbWheel`): `ScrollCore.isSidewaysWheelEvent` tells its events (a horizontal delta only, not remote-smoothed, Shift not held); at "scroll" they are smoothed as before, the distance × its speed, its "Swap direction" one more flip after "Reverse" (`flipsHorizontal`: one flip or none); with another function they are swallowed before the hand-over and the poster, and `ThumbWheelEngine` feeds `ThumbWheelStepper` (pure, the events' own timestamps): it counts events, not lines (macOS accelerates a turn's lines far beyond the thumb's movement): one unit per event, up to two for volume and zoom on a fast turn, × the speed; per function a "first" threshold after a rest or a change of direction, a "next" one while the roll goes on, and a rest; at most two steps per event (every number in `ThumbWheelTuning`, from a log of real use on 2026-11-11): a function's direction is the raw turn with "Swap direction" only, never "Reverse" or the allowlist (they depend on the app under the pointer). Its steps run their `ThumbWheelAction` on the main queue (ShortcutExecutor; `SystemVolumeKeys` for volume), never inside the callback: volume's through `ThumbWheelPacer` (pure, the time passed in; `ThumbWheelFunction.pacing`: one step at once, then one per 50 ms, at most 3 waiting and more dropped, a turn back drops the waiting ones; the system does not keep up with faster volume keys), the other functions' at once. `ScrollCore` asks `ScrollPoster.smooth(event:input:)` once per wheel event: it ends a scroll that runs for another process, or with trackpad phases when the event's scroll has none and the other way round (the setting was switched in mid-scroll, or the wheel turned sideways or back: a sideways scroll has none), makes sure a display link runs (`ScrollDisplayLink`; `ScrollCVDisplayLink` in the app, a stand-in in tests) and only then takes the event; when it does not, the event goes through as it came. A stop that closes a scroll with trackpad phases waits until its closing frames went out, for 50 ms at most (`ScrollDispatchContext.waitForQueuedFrames`; one line in the `app` log when that runs out), and a frame that opens or closes a gesture is never dropped by a later stop, only by its age (`enqueue`); without phases a stop waits for nothing and drops what still waits, as ever. `ScrollSystemLog` writes one system-log line per finished scroll, with the form it was sent in and why it ended, rate-limited passthrough reasons and one `thumb wheel:` line per roll of the sideways wheel (what it sent, its longest gap and direction changes, the travel in units, the steps that ran and those the pacer dropped; written from the main queue once it rested, at most every 2 s during a long roll). Hot path. |
| `ButtonCore/` | Two event taps that hand events to `InputProcessor`: one for the extra mouse buttons, one for keys that exists only while the keyboard is needed (`OnDemandKeyTap`: a key is bound, a button holds a modifier, a key-started action is held). `ButtonUtils.isRunnable` says which stored triggers are matched at all: a key only with ⌘ ⌥ ⌃ ⇧ or as an F-key, never the left or right button. |
| `InputEvent/` | The unified input event model, binding matching (`InputProcessor`), gesture navigation (recognizer, "follow the hand" session, pointer lock, dock swipe). `MouseInteractionSessionController` owns the pointer-movement tap: made on demand (a gesture hold that reads the pointer, a synthetic drag, a virtual modifier), paused between uses, replaced once when it is off right after it was started, released when the cores stop. |
| `Shortcut/` | System shortcut definitions and the executor of actions. `SystemVolumeKeys` presses the volume keys of an Apple keyboard (system-defined events, `post` is the seam); only the thumb wheel uses it, it is not in the action menu. `AppZoomKeys` finds the keys that type "+" (else "=") and "-" on the keyboard layout in use (Text Input Sources, UCKeyTranslate; once per layout, the layout's id compared at each step; US key codes when the layout has none; `KeyboardLayoutSource` is the seam) for the thumb wheel's ⌘-zoom; its tabs are ⌃⇥ / ⌃⇧⇥. `KeyboardLayoutKeys` finds the key of a listed letter shortcut and names keys on the layout in use (asked with Command held, once per layout); `ScriptLaunch` (in `ShortcutExecutor.swift`) starts a bound script with the responsibility disclaimed, so macOS judges it as a program of its own, and as a plain child, with a line in the `app` log, where that function is missing. |
| `Keys/` | Recording keys and mouse buttons: UI and data. `KeyRecorder` creates its event tap before it shows anything or diverts the Logitech buttons for recording; a recording ends with Esc, a plain left click (which goes on to what was clicked), when its popover closes, when the app goes to the background, or after its timeout. |
| `Logi/` | Logitech HID++: device sessions, feature discovery, button divert (`LogiDivertLedger` holds per slot what is believed diverted; the left and the right button are never diverted; after each discovery, buttons an earlier run left diverted and Scrollio does not use are given back, only while the manage switch is on, the left and right button whatever it says: `LogiDeviceSession.staleDivertDecision`), usage registry, wheel (SmartShift; `LogiWheelController` writes only values the user set, never an unset one), gesture raw XY and haptic cues, the button actions (`LogiButtonAction`; one that got no answer expires, `LogiPendingActions`), the diagnostics panel ("Logitech Diagnostics"; it writes to a mouse in Debug builds only), `LogiSystemLog` (category `logi`; a mouse is named by the model it reports, never by the name macOS has for it), `LogiTerminationSignal` (SIGTERM runs the ordinary quit, so the buttons are given back). What is written to a mouse, and when, is listed at the top of `LogiDeviceSession`. `LogiCenter` is the facade (also `controlRole(forScrollioCode:)` for the Buttons pane's drawing and `mouseDeviceStates()` with the battery). `Basics/`: battery (0x1004 / 0x1000), pointer speed (0x2201), haptic on/off and strength (0x19B0) and press force (0x19C0); one `LogiMouseBasicsController` per slot, one request at a time, byte layouts in the `*Protocol` files, public types in `LogiMouseBasicsTypes.swift`. Output reports go out through `LogiReportWriter`: one serial queue per session, off the main thread (a Bluetooth write takes 15 to 29 ms), in the order queued; answers still arrive as input reports on the main run loop; `teardown()` waits for what is queued so the mouse has its buttons back. |
| `MacPointer/` | The macOS pointer settings that apply to every mouse ("Tracking speed", "Pointer acceleration"), as System Settings › Mouse has them. `MacPointerSettings` is a set of read and write closures (`.system` in the app, `.inert` under XCTest); it writes only when the user moves the control in the Mouse pane: the HID event system property first, the global preference only if macOS took it. Scrollio stores nothing of its own here. |
| `Integration/` | The bridge between `Logi/` and the rest of the app: `LogiUsageBootstrap` pushes Options changes to `LogiCenter` (wheel, mouse basics with a 0.25 s settle, gesture tracking). |
| `Options/` | Settings in UserDefaults, per-app rules, persistence models. `Options.shared.logiMouse` holds the Logitech basics and `logiWheel` the wheel values (every value optional: nil leaves the mouse's value); `logiMouseManaged` is the one "Let Scrollio manage this mouse" switch (stored as `logiWheel.managedByScrollio`). `thumbWheel` (`ThumbWheelSettings`, key `thumbWheelSettings`, JSON read value by value) is what the sideways wheel does: function, speed, swap; global only. `ScrollSettings.swift`: the five Feel presets, the slider ranges and the clean-up that runs on every load (`ScrollSettingsCleanup`, also removes the keys of retired settings). `AppSmoothing.swift`: whether the scroll engine smooths an app and the one-click change of it for the status-bar menu; `Application+ScrollingSettings.swift`: switching "Use settings from Scrolling" on or off (the Apps pane uses it too). `SettingsFile.swift`: all settings as one JSON file (General › Settings file). It holds the allow-list of what a file has (`exportedKeys`: the UserDefaults keys are the names in the file, their values the app's own stored representation) and the list of what stays out, each with its reason (`leftOut`; never "Let Scrollio manage this mouse", never "Open at login"); the checks of a file that is read (a regular file of at most 2 MB and 1000 buttons and apps, the format's name and version, each name's type, then the same decoders as at launch; entries they cannot read and actions this version does not run are left out (`StoredAction` in `Shortcut/`), numbers of Scrolling are brought into the sliders' ranges, and both are counted for the sheet); and the import, group by group: a group the file holds is replaced in the store, then `Options.reloadFromStore` reads everything again as at launch and tells the observers of the imported groups. Nothing in it can start anything. `SettingsFileCenter.swift`: export, read and import as the pane uses them; an import first writes the current settings to a backup (`SettingsBackups`: the last five, in Application Support/<bundle id>/Settings Backups, named by their moment in UTC and only ever forward, never the same settings twice; no folder under XCTest) and afterwards calls `ScrollCore.settingsWereReplaced` (the one thing that follows no observer). |
| `Managers/` | App-level singletons: windows, the main menu, the status item (`StatusItemManager`; what its menu lists is the pure `StatusMenu`, rebuilt each time it opens: battery lines, "Smooth Scrolling in …" for the app in front, Pause / Resume, Settings, the diagnostics with Option, Quit; a low mouse battery stands next to its icon, `StatusMenu.batteryBadge`), `AppRunController` (the one path that starts and stops the event taps and the Logitech sessions: launch, sleep and wake, fast user switching, lost Accessibility access (a change of the system's Accessibility list is looked at half a second later), "Pause Scrollio" (never stored), quit; a running Mos is quit right before the taps start; taps first, Logitech only when every tap runs) and `BatteryWatcher` (one toast at low, one at critical, from what `LogiCenter` reports). |
| `Update/` | New versions (`UpdateCenter`, the only file that imports Sparkle). A copy updates itself only when its Info.plist has an update feed (`SUFeedURL`, written by `scripts/release.sh` alone) and a 32-byte public key (`SUPublicEDKey`), and it does not run from a disk image; every other copy starts no updater and makes no network request (`UpdateAvailability`). Never under XCTest (`mayStart`). The feed given to Sparkle is always the Info.plist's (`feedURLString(for:)`), not one from the preferences. The rest of the app sees `UpdateStatus` and four calls: General's "Updates" box, "Check for Updates…" in the main menu (hidden in a copy that does not update itself) and in the status menu, which starts with "Update to Scrollio …" while a found update waits. `UpdateReminder` is what shows while it waits: the Dock icon (`Utils.showDockIcon`, held past the last settings window by `Utils.dockIconIsHeld`, given back by `Utils.releaseDockIcon`) and a badge until the user looked. The order of window and icon is the center's: an asked-for find opens Sparkle's window first, as a menu bar app's (so it opens on the Space in use, over a full-screen app), and gets the icon when it starts to wait (`appResignedActive`, half a second after Scrollio left the front); a scheduled find gets the icon and the badge and no window (`standardUserDriverShouldHandleShowingScheduledUpdate` is false), and the window opens when Scrollio comes to the front (`appBecameActive`), on a click on that Dock icon (`showWaitingUpdate`, asked by `AppDelegate` before it opens the settings) or from the menu (`checkForUpdates`, which lets the icon go first when Scrollio is behind another app; so do "Install Update", `updateWillBeInstalled`, and an alert, `alertWillOpen`). One `Updates:` line per check in the `app` log. The updater's user driver is `UpdateWindows`, which forwards to Sparkle's own windows; the three outcomes Sparkle says with an app-modal alert (nothing newer, failed, installed without a relaunch) it hands on from the main run loop (`UpdateWindows.fromTheRunLoop`, a timer in the common modes) and not from the updater's main-queue block, because `runModal` inside a main-queue block stops the main dispatch queue that the event handling leans on. For an alert Scrollio comes to the front without its Dock icon (`alertWillOpen`), and "You're up to date!" has no "Version History" button. |
| `Components/` | Toast (`ToastTiming`: how long a toast stays, by the length of its text, standing still under the pointer), and `BrandTag` (the "Logi" tag of Logitech buttons in the key preview and the scroll keys). |
| `Windows/` | Introduction: the Welcome Guide, built in code, one fixed 600 × 620 window with up to four steps (Welcome, Allow Accessibility, the feel and the wheel direction, "Scrollio is ready" with Open at login, applied on Done through General's `chooseOpenAtLogin`; `IntroductionViewController` holds the bar, the transitions and the permission polling, the `WelcomeGuide*Step` files the steps and their drawings). `WelcomeGuideFlow` decides who sees what: a new user (no `optionsExist` before the first read) the whole guide at launch until it is finished (`welcomeGuideCompletedVersion`, `WelcomeGuideProgress`), anyone else only the Accessibility step when the access is missing, General › Welcome Guide the whole guide. Preferences, and the Scroll Monitor window (`MonitorWindow/`: debug build only, every file inside `#if DEBUG`; the one user of DGCharts, which the Release configuration leaves out). The Preferences window is a sidebar of panes, as in System Settings (`PreferencesSplitViewController` with `PreferencesSidebarViewController` and `PreferencesPaneHostViewController`; the pane list is `PreferencesPane.all`; `selectPane(withIdentifier:)` opens a pane); each pane sits in a 520 pt column (`PreferencesWindowLayout`), centred when narrower, and scrolls when taller than the window. Every pane is built in code (the storyboard scenes of General, Scrolling, Buttons and Apps are shells) on `PreferencesGroupedForm` (rows in rounded boxes with switch, slider and disclosure rows and slider tick marks, muted notes under the boxes, as in System Settings) and its `PreferencesListBox` (a box of selectable rows with + and − under them, as in Login Items); `PreferencesForm` keeps the window background, the column width and `fitToContent`. Buttons: a card with a drawing of the mouse (`ButtonsHeaderView`) over the list of bindings, each row with a small picture of where its button is (`ButtonsMouseMiniature`, from `ButtonTriggerPicture`: a part lit only where the place is certain, else the button's number), plain names from `ButtonTriggerNaming`, Undo for a removal; with an MX Master the rows of its buttons in the order they sit (`ButtonsListLayout`), and after the mode shift button the thumb wheel's row (`ButtonsThumbWheelRow`, a row kind of its own: no binding, its pull-down picks what the sideways wheel does, "Default" for scrolling). Scrolling: the main box, then "Thumb wheel" (what the sideways wheel does, its speed and direction, `Options.shared.thumbWheel`; one accessibility group of that name; only on the tab, not on an app's page; "Reset to Defaults" and its Undo include it; the button offers "Undo Reset" for a few seconds), "While you hold a key" (with a muted line under it while smoothing is off), then "Advanced". `ThumbWheelPresentation.swift` holds the functions' names, lines and symbols for both panes, and `ThumbWheelSettingsChange`, the one observer of `.thumbWheel` that tells the panes (they come and go; Options keeps its observers) so a change on one shows on the other. Apps: the list and a page per app (`ApplicationDetailViewController`, with the Scrolling pane embedded in per-app mode). General: a card with the app icon, name and version, with the commit and day of a build stamped by `scripts/install.sh` (`GeneralHeaderView`, in the look of the Mouse card; there is no About pane, "About Scrollio" opens General and a remembered "about" opens it too), "Scrollio is paused" with Resume (only while paused), open at login (with the approval row), show in menu bar, the Accessibility status, the updates, "Settings file" ("Export…" and "Import…", and "Restore…", an open panel on the folder of the settings kept from before each import; the save and open panels and `SettingsImportSheet` are sheets on the window, opened from the click; the sheet says what a file holds, lists every button, key and gesture with what it will do and, in a box of its own, every action that opens an app or a file or runs a script with its whole path (`SettingsFileText.visible` writes out what would not be seen), scrolls its middle part when that does not fit the screen, makes "Cancel" the button of Return unless the file binds nothing, and says why a file cannot be used), then the Welcome Guide and "Scrollio on GitHub" (`gitHubURL`, opened through the `openURL` seam) and the credit line the licence asks for (the developer tools are in the status-bar menu, with Option, not in a pane). Scrolling and Gestures have an animated preview (`ScrollPreviewView`, `GesturePreviewView`: a display link that runs only while the pane is shown and Reduce Motion is off; the pictures are in the `*Artwork` files, the timelines in the pure `*Motion` files). The mouse drawings are in `MouseView/MouseArtwork.swift`: one per model (MX Master 4 with its three side buttons and the thumb panel, MX Master 3 / 3S, a generic mouse), chosen from the connected mouse's name by `MouseArtworkModel.model(forDeviceName:)` and shared by the Mouse, Buttons and Gestures panes. `PreferencesSlider` sends nothing for a press that does not move its knob, or while it is off. |
| `Utils/` | Event tap wrapper (`Interceptor`: its keeper, the restart throttle, and which taps are off because their restarts are refused; its system calls go through `EventTapSystem`, which tests replace), `CommonModesTimer` (the safety timers: tap keepers and restarts, the recovery of `AppRunController`, the display link's keeper and rate checks; in the common run-loop modes, so they also fire while a menu is open or a modal panel runs), `AppSystemLog` (category `app`), `AppAccent` (the app's purple accent colour, the asset `AccentColor` named by the build setting `ASSETCATALOG_COMPILER_GLOBAL_ACCENT_COLOR_NAME`; macOS uses it while the user's accent is "Multicolour"; the app's drawings use `NSColor.controlAccentColor` so they follow it; its launch line in the `app` log says which accent is in effect), constants, the login item, `LegacyAppQuitter` (a running copy of the original Mos is asked to quit right before the event taps start and whenever it starts later while they run, each time with a toast, since two apps would both handle the same mouse events; while Scrollio is paused or waits for access Mos is left alone; nothing of Mos is read; an older copy of Scrollio itself is replaced the same way: a new copy waits for the old one to quit before any tap starts; only the copy that started later replaces the other), helpers. `AppLocation` (in `AppDelegate.swift`, beside the folders) asks at launch to move a copy that runs from a disk image or translocated. |
| `Extension/` | Extensions of system types. |

## Around the app

- `scripts/install.sh` (build, check and install the release app on this Mac; reads an
  optional `Local.xcconfig`), `scripts/release.sh` (the build for other people's Macs:
  universal, stripped, Developer ID, notarized, a disk image; from a commit tagged
  `v<version>`; it also makes the release an update: the feed in the build, Sparkle's
  helper programs signed, the image signed with the update key, `appcast.xml` and the
  release notes from `CHANGELOG.md`), `scripts/release-lib.sh` (its functions that can be
  tried with a development certificate; `stage_disk_image` is the one place that fills
  the disk image's folder, `make_disk_image` packs it), `scripts/dmg/` (the image's
  window: `background.tiff` with `draw-background.swift`, which draws it, and `DS_Store`,
  the layout Finder reads as `.DS_Store`, with `write-layout.swift`, which writes it, so
  that no run scripts Finder; `preview.sh` builds a sample image to look at, without a
  release; `scripts/README.md` says how to change either), `scripts/update-key.sh` (the
  update key: show, create, export, import), `scripts/qa/lint-logi-boundary.sh` (the Logi
  boundary lint, also run by a test). `ReleaseReadinessTests` reads the scripts,
  `LICENSE`, the README's acknowledgements and `Scrollio/Acknowledgements.txt` (in the app
  bundle and on the disk image) and fails when they drift apart; it also fills an image's
  folder for real and reads the layout back (the window's size, the icons' places, the
  names).
- `tools/`: hand-run HID++ probes and a standalone regression harness. Not part of any
  target; `tools/README.md` says when a probe may run.
- `assets/`: the README's pictures (`assets/README.md`). Run-time images belong in
  `Scrollio/Assets.xcassets`; the app icon is `Scrollio/AppIcon.icon` (Icon Composer).

## Boundaries

- ScrollCore is the scroll hot path: performance comes first there.
- Sparkle is known to `Scrollio/Update/` only; other code asks `UpdateCenter` and reads
  `UpdateStatus`. No other code makes a network request.
- ButtonCore, InputEvent, Shortcut and Keys together handle recording, menus, persistence
  and execution of bindings; keep their meaning of an event and an action the same.
- Logitech details stay in `Scrollio/Logi/`. Other layers use `LogiCenter`,
  `Scrollio/Integration/` or an explicit facade. `Scrollio/Integration/` may know more
  bridge types; the rest of the app only the public allowlist in
  `scripts/qa/lint-logi-boundary.sh`.
- Real-device logic is isolated behind the test plan `DebugWithDevice` (it sets
  `LOGI_REAL_DEVICE=1` for the tests).
- No setting is written to a Logitech mouse while `logiMouseManaged` is off. Not covered
  by the switch: diverting buttons (for bindings, for gestures the mouse tracks, and
  while a button is recorded), Logitech button actions, and the gesture pulse (it can
  switch the haptics on, unless the user switched haptics off in the Mouse pane). The
  Logitech Diagnostics window writes in Debug builds only. `AGENTS.md`, "Module
  boundaries"; the whole list is at the top of `LogiDeviceSession`.

## Diagnostics

The system log, subsystem `com.nikklemenc.Scrollio` (`.debug` for the debug build):
category `scroll` (`ScrollSystemLog`), category `logi` (`LogiSystemLog`, including a
summary of the writes of each connection and writes that were slow or refused) and
category `app` (`AppSystemLog` in `Utils/`: starts
and stops with their reason, event taps macOS turned off or that could not be created,
restarts and their throttle, a new copy replacing a running one). How to read it:
`docs/troubleshooting.md`, "Diagnostics". All are low volume by rule: state changes, decisions and one
line per scroll, per roll of the thumb wheel or per hold of a gesture button the mouse
tracks, never a line per wheel, pointer or key event.

## Persistence and compatibility

Be careful with these, and add a canary or compatibility test when you touch them:

- UserDefaults keys.
- Shortcut, action and category identifiers.
- Logi cache keys, feature and CID constants, usage registry constants.
- Storyboard object ids and string catalog keys.
- `welcomeGuideCompletedVersion`, the Welcome Guide's one stored value: it is settled at launch,
  before the settings are first read, from whether `optionsExist` was there.
- The settings file (`SettingsFile`): its format name and version, the six names of its
  top level, and the names of its settings, which are the UserDefaults keys. A file an
  older version wrote must stay readable. `SettingsFileFormatTests` holds the text of a
  file byte for byte, and both lists of keys: a setting the app starts to store must be
  put on one of them (in a file, or left out with a reason), or that test fails.
- The name of a settings backup (`SettingsBackups.namePrefix`): it is how the backups in
  the folder are found and put in order.
