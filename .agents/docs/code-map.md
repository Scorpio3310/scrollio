# Code map

A quick map for agents. It is not a design document: behaviour is defined by the sources
and the tests. Rules live in `AGENTS.md`.

## App (`Mos/`)

| Folder | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `ScrollCore/` | Smooth scrolling, one path: `ScrollCore` takes the wheel event (tap callback, main thread) and adds max(Step, delta) × Speed to a target; `ScrollPoster` moves toward it on every display frame (CVDisplayLink thread, under its lock) with a lerp set by Duration (`ScrollMotion`, 60 Hz reference), a lead cap (`ScrollLeadLimiter`) and a soft start (`ScrollFilter`), and posts the frames in whole pixels, the fraction of each carried into the next (`ScrollPixelCarry`; frames slower than the dead zone, a speed in pixels per 60 Hz frame, are not posted); `ScrollPhase` adds the trackpad-style phases, in one of two forms: one touch for as long as the wheel turns (AppKit and UIKit scroll views follow the touch only and compute the glide themselves), or a flick, a 45 ms touch and its glide, for an app that scrolls by the glide's events (`ScrollUtils.trackpadStyleTarget`: the Chromium engine, found by `resources.pak` in one of the app's frameworks); Safari gets no phases at all, with them it does not scroll smoothly. Trackpad (and Magic Mouse) events pass untouched: `ScrollEvent.isTrackpad` tells them by a touch or glide phase, or a swipe count on a continuous event (a wheel turned during a trackpad glide carries the count too, but is not continuous). `ScrollHandover` decides who moves the page when the two overlap: a wheel event during a trackpad glide drops the rest of that glide (its "ended" goes on), and a finger that begins to scroll stops the wheel's glide. The sideways wheel (the thumb wheel of an MX Master, or a wheel that tilts) has its own settings (`Options.thumbWheel`): `ScrollCore.isSidewaysWheelEvent` tells its events (a horizontal delta only, not remote-smoothed, Shift not held); at "scroll" they are smoothed as before, the distance × its speed, its "Swap direction" one more flip after "Reverse" (`flipsHorizontal`: one flip or none); with another function they are swallowed before the hand-over and the poster, and `ThumbWheelEngine` feeds `ThumbWheelStepper` (pure, the events' own timestamps): it counts events, not lines (macOS accelerates a turn's lines far beyond the thumb's movement): one unit per event, up to two for volume and zoom on a fast turn, × the speed; per function a "first" threshold after a rest or a change of direction, a "next" one while the roll goes on, and a rest; at most two steps per event (every number in `ThumbWheelTuning`, from a log of real use on 2026-11-11): a function's direction is the raw turn with "Swap direction" only, never "Reverse" or the allowlist (they depend on the app under the pointer). Its steps run their `ThumbWheelAction` on the main queue (ShortcutExecutor; `SystemVolumeKeys` for volume), never inside the callback: volume's through `ThumbWheelPacer` (pure, the time passed in; `ThumbWheelFunction.pacing`: one step at once, then one per 50 ms, at most 3 waiting and more dropped, a turn back drops the waiting ones; the system does not keep up with faster volume keys), the other functions' at once. `ScrollSystemLog` writes one system-log line per finished scroll, rate-limited passthrough reasons and one `thumb wheel:` line per roll of the sideways wheel (what it sent, its longest gap and direction changes, the travel in units, the steps that ran and those the pacer dropped; written from the main queue once it rested, at most every 2 s during a long roll). Hot path. |
| `ButtonCore/` | Event tap for mouse buttons and keys; hands events to `InputProcessor`. |
| `InputEvent/` | The unified input event model, binding matching (`InputProcessor`), gesture navigation (recognizer, "follow the hand" session, pointer lock, dock swipe). `MouseInteractionSessionController` owns the pointer-movement tap: made on demand (a gesture hold that reads the pointer, a synthetic drag, a virtual modifier), paused between uses, replaced once when it is off right after it was started, released when the cores stop. |
| `Shortcut/` | System shortcut definitions and the executor of actions. `SystemVolumeKeys` presses the volume keys of an Apple keyboard (system-defined events, `post` is the seam); only the thumb wheel uses it, it is not in the action menu. `AppZoomKeys` finds the keys that type "+" (else "=") and "-" on the keyboard layout in use (Text Input Sources, UCKeyTranslate; once per layout, the layout's id compared at each step; US key codes when the layout has none; `KeyboardLayoutSource` is the seam) for the thumb wheel's ⌘-zoom; its tabs are ⌃⇥ / ⌃⇧⇥. |
| `Keys/` | Recording keys and mouse buttons: UI and data. `KeyRecorder` creates its event tap before it shows anything or diverts the Logitech buttons for recording. |
| `Logi/` | Logitech HID++: device sessions, feature discovery, button divert (after each discovery, buttons an earlier run left diverted and Scrollio does not use are given back, only while the manage switch is on: `LogiDeviceSession.staleDivertDecision`), usage registry, wheel (SmartShift; `LogiWheelController` writes only values the user set, never an unset one), gesture raw XY and haptic cues, the diagnostics panel ("Logitech Diagnostics"), `LogiSystemLog` (category `logi`). `LogiCenter` is the facade (also `controlRole(forMosCode:)` for the Buttons pane's drawing and `mouseDeviceStates()` with the battery). `Basics/`: battery (0x1004 / 0x1000), pointer speed (0x2201), haptic on/off and strength (0x19B0) and press force (0x19C0); one `LogiMouseBasicsController` per slot, one request at a time, byte layouts in the `*Protocol` files, public types in `LogiMouseBasicsTypes.swift`. Output reports go out through `LogiReportWriter`: one serial queue per session, off the main thread (a Bluetooth write takes 15 to 29 ms), in the order queued; answers still arrive as input reports on the main run loop; `teardown()` waits for what is queued so the mouse has its buttons back. |
| `MacPointer/` | The macOS pointer settings that apply to every mouse ("Tracking speed", "Pointer acceleration"), as System Settings › Mouse has them. `MacPointerSettings` is a set of read and write closures (`.system` in the app, `.inert` under XCTest); it writes only when the user moves the control in the Mouse pane: the HID event system property first, the global preference only if macOS took it. Scrollio stores nothing of its own here. |
| `Integration/` | The bridge between `Logi/` and the rest of the app: `LogiUsageBootstrap` pushes Options changes to `LogiCenter` (wheel, mouse basics with a 0.25 s settle, gesture tracking). |
| `Options/` | Settings in UserDefaults, per-app rules, persistence models. `Options.shared.logiMouse` holds the Logitech basics and `logiWheel` the wheel values (every value optional: nil leaves the mouse's value); `logiMouseManaged` is the one "Let Scrollio manage this mouse" switch (stored as `logiWheel.managedByMos`). `thumbWheel` (`ThumbWheelSettings`, key `thumbWheelSettings`, JSON read value by value) is what the sideways wheel does: function, speed, swap; global only. `ScrollSettings.swift`: the five Feel presets, the slider ranges and the clean-up that runs on every load (`ScrollSettingsCleanup`, also removes the keys of retired settings). `AppSmoothing.swift`: whether the scroll engine smooths an app and the one-click change of it for the status-bar menu; `Application+ScrollingSettings.swift`: switching "Use settings from Scrolling" on or off (the Apps pane uses it too). |
| `Managers/` | App-level singletons: windows, the main menu, the status item (`StatusItemManager`; what its menu lists is the pure `StatusMenu`, rebuilt each time it opens: battery lines, "Smooth Scrolling in …" for the app in front, Pause / Resume, Settings, the diagnostics with Option, Quit; a low mouse battery stands next to its icon, `StatusMenu.batteryBadge`), `AppRunController` (the one path that starts and stops the event taps and the Logitech sessions: launch, sleep and wake, fast user switching, lost Accessibility access, "Pause Scrollio" (never stored), quit; taps first, Logitech only when every tap runs) and `BatteryWatcher` (one toast at low, one at critical, from what `LogiCenter` reports). |
| `Components/` | Toast, and `BrandTag` (the "Logi" tag of Logitech buttons in the key preview and the scroll keys). |
| `Windows/` | Introduction: the Welcome Guide, built in code, one fixed 600 × 620 window with up to four steps (Welcome, Allow Accessibility, the feel and the wheel direction, "Scrollio is ready" with Open at login, applied on Done through General's `chooseOpenAtLogin`; `IntroductionViewController` holds the bar, the transitions and the permission polling, the `WelcomeGuide*Step` files the steps and their drawings). `WelcomeGuideFlow` decides who sees what: a new user (no `optionsExist` before the first read) the whole guide at launch until it is finished (`welcomeGuideCompletedVersion`, `WelcomeGuideProgress`), anyone else only the Accessibility step when the access is missing, General › Welcome Guide the whole guide. Preferences and the Monitor window. The Preferences window is a sidebar of panes, as in System Settings (`PreferencesSplitViewController` with `PreferencesSidebarViewController` and `PreferencesPaneHostViewController`; the pane list is `PreferencesPane.all`; `selectPane(withIdentifier:)` opens a pane); each pane sits in a 520 pt column (`PreferencesWindowLayout`), centred when narrower, and scrolls when taller than the window. Every pane is built in code (the storyboard scenes of General, Scrolling, Buttons and Apps are shells) on `PreferencesGroupedForm` (rows in rounded boxes with switch, slider and disclosure rows and slider tick marks, muted notes under the boxes, as in System Settings) and its `PreferencesListBox` (a box of selectable rows with + and − under them, as in Login Items); `PreferencesForm` keeps the window background, the column width and `fitToContent`. Buttons: a card with a drawing of the mouse (`ButtonsHeaderView`) over the list of bindings, each row with a small picture of where its button is (`ButtonsMouseMiniature`, from `ButtonTriggerPicture`: a part lit only where the place is certain, else the button's number), plain names from `ButtonTriggerNaming`, Undo for a removal; with an MX Master the rows of its buttons in the order they sit (`ButtonsListLayout`), and after the mode shift button the thumb wheel's row (`ButtonsThumbWheelRow`, a row kind of its own: no binding, its pull-down picks what the sideways wheel does, "Default" for scrolling). Scrolling: the main box, then "Thumb wheel" (what the sideways wheel does, its speed and direction, `Options.shared.thumbWheel`; one accessibility group of that name; only on the tab, not on an app's page; "Reset to Defaults" and its Undo include it), "While you hold a key", then "Advanced". `ThumbWheelPresentation.swift` holds the functions' names, lines and symbols for both panes, and `ThumbWheelSettingsChange`, the one observer of `.thumbWheel` that tells the panes (they come and go; Options keeps its observers) so a change on one shows on the other. Apps: the list and a page per app (`ApplicationDetailViewController`, with the Scrolling pane embedded in per-app mode). General: a card with the app icon, name and version, with the commit and day of a build stamped by `scripts/install.sh` (`GeneralHeaderView`, in the look of the Mouse card; there is no About pane, "About Scrollio" opens General and a remembered "about" opens it too), "Scrollio is paused" with Resume (only while paused), open at login (with the approval row), show in menu bar, the Accessibility status, then the Welcome Guide and "Scrollio on GitHub" (`gitHubURL`, opened through the `openURL` seam) and the credit line the licence asks for (the developer tools are in the status-bar menu, with Option, not in a pane). Scrolling and Gestures have an animated preview (`ScrollPreviewView`, `GesturePreviewView`: a display link that runs only while the pane is shown and Reduce Motion is off; the pictures are in the `*Artwork` files, the timelines in the pure `*Motion` files). The mouse drawings are in `MouseView/MouseArtwork.swift`: one per model (MX Master 4 with its three side buttons and the thumb panel, MX Master 3 / 3S, a generic mouse), chosen from the connected mouse's name by `MouseArtworkModel.model(forDeviceName:)` and shared by the Mouse, Buttons and Gestures panes. `PreferencesSlider` sends nothing for a press that does not move its knob, or while it is off. |
| `Utils/` | Event tap wrapper (`Interceptor`: its keeper, the restart throttle, and which taps are off because their restarts are refused), `CommonModesTimer` (the safety timers: tap keepers and restarts, the recovery of `AppRunController`, the display link's keeper and rate checks; in the common run-loop modes, so they also fire while a menu is open or a modal panel runs), `AppSystemLog` (category `app`), `AppAccent` (the app's purple accent colour, the asset `AccentColor` named by the build setting `ASSETCATALOG_COMPILER_GLOBAL_ACCENT_COLOR_NAME`; macOS uses it while the user's accent is "Multicolour"; the app's drawings use `NSColor.controlAccentColor` so they follow it; its launch line in the `app` log says which accent is in effect), constants, the login item, the takeover from Mos and from an older copy of Scrollio itself (a new copy waits for the old one to quit before any tap starts), helpers. |
| `Extension/` | Extensions of system types. |

Tests are in `MosTests/`; Logitech test doubles in `MosTests/LogiTestDoubles/`.

## Around the app

- `scripts/qa/lint-logi-boundary.sh`: the Logi boundary lint (also run by a test).
- `tools/`: hand-run HID++ probes and a standalone regression harness. Not part of any
  target; `tools/README.md` says when a probe may run.
- `assets/`: the README's pictures (`assets/README.md`). Run-time images belong in
  `Mos/Assets.xcassets`; the app icon is `Mos/AppIcon.icon` (Icon Composer).

## Boundaries

- ScrollCore is the scroll hot path: performance comes first there.
- ButtonCore, InputEvent, Shortcut and Keys together handle recording, menus, persistence
  and execution of bindings; keep their meaning of an event and an action the same.
- Logitech details stay in `Mos/Logi/`. Other layers use `LogiCenter`, `Mos/Integration/`
  or an explicit facade. `Mos/Integration/` may know more bridge types; the rest of the
  app only the public allowlist in `scripts/qa/lint-logi-boundary.sh`.
- Real-device logic is isolated behind `LOGI_REAL_DEVICE=1`.
- Nothing is written to a Logitech mouse while `logiMouseManaged` is off (gesture cues may
  still switch its haptics on, unless the user switched haptics off in the Mouse pane).

## Diagnostics

The system log, subsystem `com.nikklemenc.Scrollio` (`.debug` for the debug build):
category `scroll` (`ScrollSystemLog`), category `logi` (`LogiSystemLog`, including a
summary of the writes of each connection and writes that were slow or refused) and
category `app` (`AppSystemLog` in `Utils/`: starts
and stops with their reason, event taps macOS turned off or that could not be created,
restarts and their throttle, a new copy replacing a running one). How to read it:
`README.md`, "Diagnostics". All are low volume by rule: state changes, decisions and one
line per scroll, never a line per event.

## Persistence and compatibility

Be careful with these, and add a canary or compatibility test when you touch them:

- UserDefaults keys.
- Shortcut, action and category identifiers.
- Logi cache keys, feature and CID constants, usage registry constants.
- Storyboard object ids and string catalog keys.
- The first-launch copy of the settings of the original Mos (`Utils/LegacyMosTakeover.swift`).
- `welcomeGuideCompletedVersion`, the Welcome Guide's one stored value: it is settled at launch,
  before the settings are first read, from whether `optionsExist` was there.
